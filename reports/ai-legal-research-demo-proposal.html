AI Legal Research Assistant

A pilot demo showing how a domain-tuned AI agent accelerates case-law research, citation checking, and brief drafting for litigation teams.

1. Executive Summary

Legal teams spend 30–40% of billable hours on research — locating precedent, validating citations, and synthesizing memos. This proposal outlines a 2-week demo of an AI legal research assistant that ingests a firm's matter docket and a curated jurisdictional corpus, then answers natural-language research queries with grounded citations.

Goal: prove a 3× research speedup on representative matters with zero hallucinated citations.

2. Problem

  • Associates duplicate research across matters; institutional knowledge is trapped in PDFs.
  • Generic LLMs hallucinate case names and reporter numbers — unacceptable for filings.
  • Westlaw / Lexis bills meter per query; broad exploration is cost-prohibitive.

3. Proposed Solution

Retrieval Layer

Hybrid BM25 + dense vector search over a jurisdiction-scoped corpus (CourtListener, Caselaw Access, firm memos).

Reasoning Layer

Claude Sonnet 4.6 with tool-use: cite-checker, Bluebook formatter, parallel-citation resolver.

Verification

Every citation re-fetched and span-matched against source; unverifiable claims flagged inline.

Interface

Chat UI with side-panel source viewer, exportable memo draft, matter-scoped history.

8. Risk & Mitigation

  • Hallucination: Citation verifier blocks unverifiable claims; user sees source span.
  • Privilege leakage: Firm corpus stays in tenant-isolated index; no training on inputs.
  • Adoption: Pilot includes 2 attorney working sessions for hands-on onboarding.
